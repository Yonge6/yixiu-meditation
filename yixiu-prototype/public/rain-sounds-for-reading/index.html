A simple reading setup

Let the rain stay behind the book

The sound is not the reading ritual. It is only a quiet edge around the page. Make three decisions, then stop adjusting.

  1. 01

    Open the page first

    Choose the chapter, article or document before pressing play. When the next sentence is already visible, the sound can mark a beginning instead of becoming another thing to browse.

  2. 02

    Use the lowest useful volume

    Set the rain just high enough to soften nearby movement or conversation. The words should remain more noticeable than individual drops.

  3. 03

    Choose one finish line

    Read one chapter or use a 15-, 30- or 60-minute timer. Keep the sound and volume unchanged until that boundary arrives.

Choose by texture

Light rain, window rain or flowing water?

There is no universal best background sound for reading. Choose the texture that asks the least of your attention in this room, with this book.

  1. 01

Rain instead of a playlist

Protect the language channel

A familiar song can invite memory, prediction or silent singing. Spoken audio brings another stream of words. Light rain does neither, but preference still matters.

  1. 01

    Use it for language-heavy work

    Try rain for novels, essays, research papers, proofreading or writing—moments when another voice can compete with the words in front of you.

  2. 02

    Pause it when sound is the task

    If you are learning pronunciation, editing audio or reading aloud, silence may serve you better. A background sound should never make the task harder to hear.

  3. 03

    Keep one repeatable cue

    Using the same scene at the beginning of a reading block can make the first step smaller: open the page, start the rain, read the next line.

Before you read

Rain sounds for reading: common questions

Does this rain sound include music, talking or thunder?

No. The preview is a real light-rain recording without music, spoken guidance or added thunder.

How loud should rain be while I read?

Use the lowest comfortable level that softens the room while leaving the words in front of you clear. If you start following individual raindrops, lower the volume.

Is rain better than music for reading?

It depends on the reader and the book. Rain has no lyrics or melody to follow, which some readers prefer when the text itself needs their language attention.

Can the rain continue while I read in another iPhone app?

Yes. With background playback enabled in Yixiu, the selected rain scene can continue while you use a reading, notes or writing app.
