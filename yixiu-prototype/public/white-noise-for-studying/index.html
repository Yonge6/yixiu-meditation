A simpler study setup

Use white noise as a boundary, not the task

White noise does not help everyone focus. Use it only when it makes the room easier to ignore, and choose silence when the sound itself begins to take attention.

  1. 01

    Open one real assignment

    Put the chapter, worksheet or draft in front of you before pressing play. The sound should support a defined next action rather than another browsing session.

  2. 02

    Start at the lowest useful volume

    Raise the level only until nearby conversation or movement feels softer. Your work should remain more noticeable than the white noise.

  3. 03

    Choose one finish line

    Complete one section or set a 15-, 30- or 60-minute timer. Keep the sound unchanged until that boundary arrives.

A fair one-block test

Keep it only if the task becomes easier to notice

Preference matters more than a universal rule. Compare two short blocks instead of deciding from the first few seconds.

  1. 01

    Match the task

    Use the same kind of work for both conditions, such as two pages of reading or two similar practice problems.

  2. 02

    Try white noise, then silence

    Keep each block short and do not change anything else. Notice interruptions, not whether the sound feels impressive.

  3. 03

    Keep the quieter condition

    If the white noise becomes something you monitor, pause it. A useful background should ask less of you, not more.

Before you begin

White noise for studying: common questions

Does white noise help everyone study?

No. Some people use a steady sound to soften nearby distractions, while others study better in silence. Try one short block each way and keep the condition that feels less distracting.

Does this study white noise include music or talking?

No. The preview contains Yixiu's deep underwater white-noise texture without music, lyrics, narration or spoken prompts.

What volume should I use while studying?

Begin at the lowest comfortable level that softens unpredictable room noise. Your assignment should remain more noticeable than the sound.

Can white noise keep playing while I use another iPhone app?
